PRD — AI-Enabled Scholarship & Fellowship Management System (MoTA)

SIH 2026 · PS 26239 · Ministry of Tribal Affairs · Category: Software · Theme: Smart Education Prepared 29 Sep 2026. Status: working draft for team kickoff.

How to read this. Sections 1–4 are grounded in the official PS text and the Ministry's own pages. Section 12 is grounded in what I read in public repos. Everything marked [Proposed] or [Assumption] is my recommendation, not a fact about MoTA. Section 15 lists what I could not verify.

1. The PS in one page

Ask: one common, secure, transparent, intelligent digital platform that runs MoTA's scholarship and fellowship schemes for Scheduled Tribe (ST) students end to end — apply → verify → scrutinise → screen → select → communicate → post-selection management — with scheme-specific eligibility, selection and documents configured (not hard-coded).

Named schemes in the PS: NFST (research fellowships in India) and NOS (Master's/PhD abroad). MoTA runs three more (Section 2), and "configurable" is the hint that the platform should not be limited to two.

Problems the PS names: manual scrutiny, repeated correspondence, multiple verification levels, processing delays, repetitive admin effort, limited real-time visibility, errors in verification and workflow.

The five "Expected Solution" bullets (your scoring rubric):

# Official bullet What it really means
E1 End-to-end digital application management Submission, upload, eligibility check, scrutiny, selection, communication in one system
E2 Automate eligibility + document verification with configurable scheme rules and AI (OCR / document intelligence) Rules engine driven by data + OCR pipeline
E3 Transparent screening/selection using approved criteria, merit-based, with human oversight Explainable scoring; AI never decides alone
E4 Application tracking + workflow for applicants and admins, incl. deficiency identification, communication, resubmission Deficiency loop is a first-class feature
E5 Dashboards and analytics for the Ministry Monitoring of applications, verification, selection, scheme performance

Other constraints in the text: separate interfaces for applicants and administrators; "AI-based tools wherever appropriate" (not everywhere); improve accuracy, transparency and accountability. Video link: NA. Data link set: only two public MoTA pages (no dataset is provided).

2. Domain context

2.1 The five MoTA schemes (from tribal.nic.in and dbttribal.gov.in)

Scheme (DBT code) Type Who administers Income cap Key benefit / selection
Pre-Matric (BPVGK) Centrally Sponsored States/UTs via State portal or NSP ≤ ₹2.5 L Classes IX–X; ₹225/mo day scholar, ₹525/mo hosteller, 10 months
Post-Matric (BVOBC) Centrally Sponsored States/UTs via State portal or NSP ≤ ₹2.5 L Class X+ recognised courses; compulsory fees + maintenance ₹230–1,200/mo
Top Class Education (A023B) Central Sector Centre, via scholarships.gov.in ≤ ₹6 L Listed premier institutes (IITs, IIMs, AIIMS, NITs…); tuition, living, book/computer allowances
NFST (ARG45) Central Sector Centre, fellowship.tribal.gov.in Not stated on page M.Phil/PhD; 750 fresh awards/yr; merit on Master's marks; preference to girls, Divyang, PVTGs
NOS (AZKMI) Central Sector Centre, overseas.tribal.gov.in ≤ ₹6 L PG/PhD/post-doc abroad; interview-based merit by Expert Committee; 17 ST + 3 PVTG awards/yr; paid via Indian Missions (MEA)

Scale: the Ministry says about 30 lakh ST students are covered through DBT.

Design implications

  • Pre/Post-Matric are state-run and reach the Ministry as beneficiary/fund data via the DBT portal. For these the platform's role is closer to monitoring and interoperability than direct scrutiny. NFST/NOS/Top Class are where a direct workflow adds most value.
  • NOS is not paid like the others. The DBT portal lists its benefit type as "In Others", and the site says money goes through Indian Missions abroad. A generic "release installment to bank account" module will not model it. Make the disbursement channel a per-scheme setting.
  • The Ministry's own pages disagree with each other and with several repos (Section 12.4). That is the strongest argument for your configurable-rules design and a reason to cite the guideline PDFs by version.

2.2 The current landscape you must fit into

  • Existing portals: fellowship.tribal.gov.in, overseas.tribal.gov.in, scholarships.gov.in (NSP), dbttribal.gov.in, plus a grievance module.
  • NFST/NOS manuals already mention DigiLocker, UMANG and Canara Bank linkage and a verification module. So "no digital process today" is the wrong framing. Frame your product as one orchestration layer that removes re-verification and dead-end rejections.
  • Grievance load is evidence of friction: as of 30 Jun 2021 the NFST portal had 17,638 student grievances received (17,580 solved), against 430 for NOS and 1,453 for Top Class. (Dated figures; use as illustration.)

3. Goals, non-goals, metrics

Goals

  1. One platform, many schemes, configured by admins without code changes.
  2. Cut manual scrutiny by automating checks and routing only exceptions to humans.
  3. Make every outcome explainable and auditable.
  4. Give applicants live status and a clear route to fix deficiencies.
  5. Give the Ministry a real-time picture of pipeline and scheme performance.

Non-goals (say these out loud in the pitch)

  • AI does not approve, reject or rank on its own authority. It extracts, checks, flags, explains.
  • No claim of live integration with Aadhaar, DigiLocker, PFMS or NSP. Use documented adapters with mocks.
  • Not replacing state portals for Pre/Post-Matric.

Success metrics [Proposed — validate baselines with the Ministry; do not present as facts]

Metric Target for demo
Field-extraction accuracy on your test set of certificates ≥ 90 % on key fields, with per-field confidence shown
Applications auto-cleared with no human touch vs routed to review Report the split honestly, per scheme
Deficiency detected at upload time vs at scrutiny Majority caught at upload
Decisions with stored reason + actor + timestamp 100 %
Time to add a new scheme via config Minutes, live in demo, zero deploy

5. End-to-end lifecycle

stateDiagram-v2
    [*] --> Draft
    Draft --> Submitted
    Submitted --> AutoScreened
    AutoScreened --> Deficient: missing or mismatched
    Deficient --> Resubmitted: applicant cures
    Resubmitted --> AutoScreened
    AutoScreened --> UnderScrutiny
    UnderScrutiny --> Eligible
    UnderScrutiny --> Ineligible: reason mandatory
    Ineligible --> Appealed
    Appealed --> UnderScrutiny
    Eligible --> Shortlisted
    Shortlisted --> Selected
    Shortlisted --> Waitlisted
    Shortlisted --> NotSelected
    Selected --> Sanctioned
    Sanctioned --> Disbursing
    Disbursing --> Active
    Active --> Completed

Design rules: every transition is logged (actor, time, reason, before/after); only permitted roles can trigger each transition; rejections require a stated reason; deficiency has a configurable cure window.

6. Functional requirements

Priority: P0 must show in demo · P1 should · P2 if time.

M1 Identity and applicant profile

  • FR-1.1 (P0) Register/login with mobile or email OTP; JWT session with refresh.
  • FR-1.2 (P0) One reusable applicant profile (personal, category, education, bank) so data and documents are entered once.
  • FR-1.3 (P1) DigiLocker-style document fetch via adapter (mock).
  • FR-1.4 (P1) Masked identity handling: never store a full Aadhaar number in plain form.

M2 Scheme discovery and pre-check

  • FR-2.1 (P0) Public scheme catalogue rendered from configuration.
  • FR-2.2 (P0) Eligibility pre-check with criterion-by-criterion pass/fail and suggested alternative schemes.
  • FR-2.3 (P1) Personalised scheme recommendation from the profile.

M3 Configurable scheme engine (the core differentiator)

  • FR-3.1 (P0) Scheme → versions → stages → rules → form fields → document requirements → scoring model, all stored as data.
  • FR-3.2 (P0) Rule types: threshold, range, set membership, conditional, composite (AND/OR), hard vs soft severity, effective dates.
  • FR-3.3 (P0) Dry-run sandbox: test a changed rule against existing applications and show pass-rate change before publishing.
  • FR-3.4 (P0) Versioning: an application is evaluated under the version in force when it was submitted; publishing needs a reason and is audited.
  • FR-3.5 (P1) Approval step (maker–checker) for publishing rule changes.
  • FR-3.6 (P1) Version diff view.
  • FR-3.7 (P2) Full no-code builder UI for adding a brand-new scheme.

M4 Application management

  • FR-4.1 (P0) Dynamic multi-step form generated from scheme configuration; autosave drafts.
  • FR-4.2 (P0) Document upload with type, size, magic-byte and integrity checks; SHA-256 stored.
  • FR-4.3 (P0) Submission generates an application ID and acknowledgement (PDF).
  • FR-4.4 (P0) Live status timeline visible to the applicant.

M5 Document intelligence

  • FR-5.1 (P0) Quality guard on upload: blur, contrast, crop/skew, unreadable file, wrong page.
  • FR-5.2 (P0) Document classification (caste, income, marksheet, ID, admission letter, etc.) to catch wrong-slot uploads.
  • FR-5.3 (P0) OCR and structured field extraction with per-field confidence.
  • FR-5.4 (P0) Cross-checks: form vs document, document vs document, certificate validity dates.
  • FR-5.5 (P1) Fuzzy name matching that tolerates transliteration variants (edit distance plus phonetic).
  • FR-5.6 (P1) Duplicate and tamper signals: identical file hash, repeated certificate numbers, reused documents across applicants.
  • FR-5.7 (P0) Every flag carries an explanation the applicant can understand ("You entered ₹3,00,000; certificate shows ₹4,50,000").

M6 Eligibility and scrutiny

  • FR-6.1 (P0) Rules engine outputs pass/fail per rule with the evidence used.
  • FR-6.2 (P0) Two routes: clean applications fast-tracked to officer confirmation; flagged ones to a review queue.
  • FR-6.3 (P0) Side-by-side viewer (document image, extracted values, flags) with approve / reject / raise-deficiency.
  • FR-6.4 (P0) Human confirmation and written remarks required for every ineligibility decision.
  • FR-6.5 (P1) Multi-level scrutiny (verifier → officer) configurable per scheme.

M7 Deficiency and communication

  • FR-7.1 (P0) Deficiency notice states exactly what is wrong, what to upload, and the deadline.
  • FR-7.2 (P0) Applicant inbox with countdown, single-item re-upload, automatic re-screening.
  • FR-7.3 (P0) Notifications on each status change (in-app plus email/SMS via adapter; bilingual templates).
  • FR-7.4 (P1) Deadline reminders and auto-escalation when a cure window lapses.

M8 Screening and selection

  • FR-8.1 (P0) Merit scoring model defined in configuration (components and weights per scheme). Do not hard-code weights.
  • FR-8.2 (P0) Preferences and quotas applied as configured (for example girls, Divyang, PVTG for NFST; 17+3 split for NOS).
  • FR-8.3 (P0) Score breakdown visible to officers and, in summary, to applicants.
  • FR-8.4 (P1) Committee scoring: interview marks (NOS) and research proposal marks (NFST-style).
  • FR-8.5 (P0) Publish merit list, selected and waitlist, triggering notifications.
  • FR-8.6 (P0) Any override requires written justification and is audited.
  • FR-8.7 (P1) Waitlist promotion when a selected candidate declines.

M9 Post-selection management

  • FR-9.1 (P1) Acceptance / joining confirmation with institute verification.
  • FR-9.2 (P1) Sanction order generation (PDF).
  • FR-9.3 (P1) Disbursement tracking with a per-scheme channel (bank/DBT batch vs via-mission for NOS), status and reconciliation view via mock PFMS adapter.
  • FR-9.4 (P2) Periodic progress or attendance certification and renewal.
  • FR-9.5 (P1) Grievance and appeal workflow.

M10 Dashboards and analytics

  • FR-10.1 (P0) Executive dashboard: applications funnel, turnaround time, deficiency reasons, scheme-wise status, state-wise map.
  • FR-10.2 (P1) Equity view: gender, PVTG, state and institute-type distribution of applicants vs selected.
  • FR-10.3 (P1) Officer workload and queue ageing.
  • FR-10.4 (P1) Anomaly view (duplicates, outliers). CSV and PDF export.

M11 Platform

  • FR-11.1 (P0) RBAC with the roles above.
  • FR-11.2 (P0) Tamper-evident audit log (hash-chained) with a verify-integrity screen.
  • FR-11.3 (P0) English + Hindi UI; language persists. (P2: a few regional languages.)
  • FR-11.4 (P0) Accessibility: keyboard navigation, contrast, font scaling.
  • FR-11.5 (P1) Helpdesk chatbot grounded on scheme documents (retrieval, no free-form answers on eligibility decisions).
  • FR-11.6 (P0) Public OpenAPI docs.

7. AI design: what AI does and does not do

Task Approach [Proposed] Decides?
Image quality check OpenCV (Laplacian variance for blur, histogram contrast, skew) No, gives feedback
Document classification Keyword/layout heuristics first, small classifier or LLM as fallback No
OCR Tesseract or PaddleOCR (English + Hindi), with OpenCV preprocessing No
Field extraction Regex/templates first; LLM structured extraction as optional fallback No
Name matching Levenshtein + phonetic + token scoring Flags only
Eligibility Deterministic rules engine Computes; officer confirms
Merit ranking Deterministic weighted model from config Computes; committee approves
Anomaly / fraud Hash duplicates, repeated IDs, statistical outliers Flags only
Chatbot Retrieval over scheme docs Answers FAQs only

Principles: (1) the LLM never sees the decision path; (2) a fully working offline/no-key mode so a demo never dies on Wi-Fi; (3) confidence thresholds route uncertain cases to humans; (4) data sovereignty — these are ST students' identity and income documents. Prefer on-prem OCR by default and send nothing to an external LLM without redaction and a clear switch. This is my judgment, but government reviewers tend to care about it.

8. Data model (core entities)

Applicant · ApplicantEducation · Scheme → SchemeVersion → Stage → Rule · FormField · DocumentRequirement · ScoringModel · Application (pinned to a SchemeVersion) · ApplicationDocument (hash, type, OCR result, confidence) · RuleEvaluation (rule, result, evidence) · Deficiency (items, deadline, status) · Review (actor, decision, remarks) · MeritEntry (components, score, rank, quota bucket) · Sanction · Disbursement (channel, status, reference) · Notification · Grievance · AuditLog (prev-hash, hash) · User / Role.

Store rule definitions as versioned JSON (relational core, JSON for rule bodies).

10. Non-functional requirements

  • Security: password hashing, OTP with expiry and attempt limits, rate limiting, input validation, upload hardening (type, magic bytes, size, path-traversal safe, random file names), least-privilege RBAC, secrets only in environment variables, no PII in logs.
  • Privacy: DPDP Act 2023 principles — consent, purpose limitation, minimisation, retention rules. Encrypt documents at rest in production.
  • Auditability: append-only, hash-chained log; every override and rule change has actor, reason, timestamp, IP.
  • Accessibility and inclusion: WCAG 2.1 AA, low-bandwidth friendly, mobile-first, bilingual; consider assisted-mode for CSC / village kiosks [Assumption: rural applicants are a core segment].
  • Scale [Assumption]: design for lakhs of applications per cycle; async OCR via a job queue; pagination everywhere.
  • Reliability: deterministic offline mode for AI features; graceful degradation.
  • Hosting note: production would sit on NIC or a MeitY-empanelled cloud; say so in the deck.

12.2 Patterns nearly everyone converged on

  1. Data-driven rules (rules stored as data, editable by admin) — usually with a test sandbox.
  2. OCR + classification + cross-check of form vs document, with a confidence score.
  3. Deficiency loop with a cure window (7 or 15 days) and re-upload.
  4. "AI advises, human decides" with a mandatory-remarks audit trail.
  5. Weighted merit score plus quotas/preferences.
  6. Mock DBT/PFMS/APBS disbursement.
  7. Role-based portals, bilingual UI, dashboards, chatbot.

Implication: these are table stakes. Doing all seven well will make you equal, not better.

12.3 Ideas worth borrowing

  • Dry-run of rule changes against real applications (#1).
  • Transliteration-tolerant tribal-name matching (#15) — solves a real cause of wrongful rejection.
  • Upload-time quality guard so bad photos never reach a human (#15).
  • Deficiency as "cure, not reject", including SMS/WhatsApp-style links (#15).
  • Explicit mock/real toggle and no-key operation (#2, #12, #20).
  • Granular institutional roles: institute, district, state (#2).
  • Document-state matrix: mandatory / conditional / waived / not applicable (#29).
  • Automated tests and CI as a credibility signal (#20).
  • A written judge demo script (#1, #15).

12.4 Red flags and inconsistencies to avoid

  • Numbers disagree with the Ministry's pages. Several repos say NFST has "750+ seats, ₹32,000/mo" and NOS "100 seats"; the Ministry's page lists NFST ₹25,000 (M.Phil) / ₹28,000 (PhD) and 20 NOS awards. The official page may be older than the repos' sources, or the repos may be wrong. One repo says NFST needs UGC-NET/JRF while the page says merit on Master's marks. Treat all as unresolved until you check the current guideline PDFs, and put the source and date on each rule.
  • Invented merit weights. Three repos use three different formulas (50/30/20; 25/30/15/10/20; 40/20/20/20). None cites a guideline. The PS says "approved scheme criteria", so make weights configurable and label demo weights as placeholders.
  • Suspiciously perfect ML metrics (99 % accuracy, 100 % ROC-AUC, 0.999 R²) and "100 % rule accuracy" come from synthetic data generated by the same rules. That is label leakage, not evidence. A judge who asks "how was this trained?" will end that pitch.
  • Fabricated impact stats on landing pages (for example crores disbursed). Use clearly labelled illustrative numbers or none.
  • Secrets and credentials in READMEs, including one real database password. Never commit secrets; a "secure government system" that leaks its own passwords loses credibility fast.
  • Sending PII to external LLM APIs with no redaction discussion.
  • Near-duplicate submissions. Repos #1/#3 and #19/#20 share templates. Expect judges to have seen the "rule builder + OCR + merit + fraud + audit" demo many times.
  • Also: dependence on in-memory storage in a demo (#20 admits it), and a 30-second demo that only works with hand-picked sample files.

13. How to differentiate [Proposed]

  1. Evidence-first decisions. Show the exact region of the document that produced each extracted value, and store it with the rule result. This turns "AI extracted it" into "here's the proof".
  2. Impact-aware rule publishing. Sandbox plus a diff and an equity impact preview (how does this rule change the pass rate for PVTG, women, each state?). Nobody surveyed does the fairness part.
  3. Cure-not-reject UX for first-generation applicants: plain-language deficiency notices in Hindi/English, one-item re-upload, mobile-first, assisted mode.
  4. Verifiable audit chain: a screen where an auditor can re-verify the hash chain live.
  5. Sovereign-by-default AI: offline OCR path, LLM optional and redacted, provider switch in config.
  6. Interop contracts: documented adapters plus contract tests, so "integration" is a claim you can back.
  7. Scheme onboarding demo: create a new scheme (for example a hypothetical state-specific scheme) from the admin UI in under five minutes with zero code.

15. Assumptions, open questions, unverified items

Open questions to resolve first

  1. Which schemes must be live in the demo: NFST + NOS only, or all five?
  2. Current guideline values (income caps, stipends, seats, NET/JRF requirement, age limits, quota percentages). Pull from the latest PDFs on tribal.nic.in and record version and date.
